# Lutz Consulting Group consulting site — current specification

**Status:** active design direction  
**Last reconciled:** July 18, 2026  
**Review route:** `/consulting/prototypes/editorial`  
**Detailed work route:** `/consulting/prototypes/editorial/work`

## 1. Product statement

The consulting site should make Lutz Consulting Group feel like one accountable partner for the digital systems behind a business. The website is the easiest entry point, but the relationship can extend through AI integrations, analytics, operations, product work, and ongoing care.

The site is not selling “AI-generated websites,” a fixed stack, or isolated technical tasks. It is selling judgment, continuity, and ownership across the tools a business depends on.

### Core promise

> Don’t let your website become your second job.

> We design, build, and operate the digital systems behind your business—so you can keep running the business.

This language is intentionally concrete enough to understand in one pass and broad enough to cover websites, AI integrations, analytics, and adjacent operational work.

## 2. Business goals

The site must:

- Make a recruiter, warm lead, or curious visitor think the work is unusually considered and technically credible.
- Turn a broad consulting practice into a simple entry story: begin with the website or digital product, then connect and improve the systems around it.
- Establish Lutz Consulting Group as a scalable firm by using “we,” with no founder biography or personal-first copy on the consulting pages.
- Convert visitors through email now, without requiring a polished brief or a predefined package.
- Support both project work and longer relationships. A rebuild followed by recurring care is the preferred relationship, but neither standalone care nor small paid tasks should be prohibited.
- Show real products and live links instead of generic mock websites or invented results.

## 3. Audience

Primary visitors are:

- Potential warm-lead clients who encounter the work through another project or referral.
- Business owners whose websites, commerce systems, data, or automations have become operational burdens.
- Recruiters and technical peers evaluating product sense, engineering judgment, and visual taste.
- Current networks in dance and trading, without making either industry the limits of the practice.

The reader is assumed to understand a business problem, not infrastructure jargon. Copy should describe what changes for the business before naming implementation details.

## 4. Offer hierarchy

The three public offers, in order, are:

1. **Websites, built with judgment**  
   Design, development, rebuilds, commerce migrations, custom products, and integrations. Every release is read, understood, tested, and owned by a person.

2. **AI that fits the work**  
   AI integrations, internal tools, workflow automation, and agents placed inside the business a client already runs. Human checkpoints remain where judgment matters.

3. **Analytics that leads somewhere**  
   Measurement, monitoring, reporting, experiments, and growth work tied to real decisions. LCG can monitor and act continuously or provide reports when that is what the client wants.

Websites lead because they are the clearest, easiest-to-buy entry service. AI and analytics demonstrate that the relationship extends beyond a page launch. Operations and machine learning remain legitimate capabilities but do not need separate top-level categories yet.

### Commercial posture

- Preferred path: rebuild or focused build → launch → recurring care and improvement.
- Also acceptable: existing-system care, standalone analytics or AI work, and small paid tasks.
- Do not publish a minimum engagement or imply that small work is unwelcome.
- Do not reduce the practice to maintenance. Maintenance is part of ownership, not the headline.

## 5. Brand posture and voice

- Brand name: **Lutz Consulting Group**.
- Voice: first-person plural (“we”), calm, direct, technically literate, and human.
- Do not mention Jack or include a founder section on the consulting site.
- Do not describe the work as “vibecoding,” even when contrasting it with self-serve tools.
- Be comfortable acknowledging modern tools: “We use some of these tools too.”
- The differentiator is not hand coding versus AI. It is accountable human judgment across the complete system.
- Avoid inflated agency language, invented scale, generic “transform your business” claims, or unexplained technical superiority.

## 6. Chosen information architecture

The chosen architecture is a focused landing page plus a separate visual case-study page. This follows the pacing of In the Brand Lab: the landing page makes the argument; the work page rewards deeper investigation.

### Landing page order

1. **Fixed navigation**  
   LCG mark and name; Services, Work, Contact; Start a project.

2. **Hero and browser ownership story**  
   Headline, positioning copy, two CTAs, and one large animated browser. The browser begins inside the first viewport; a short scroll reveals the complete stage and its centered five-beat timing rail. The animation is the opening visual, not a separate full-screen chapter farther down the page.

3. **Three sequential service chapters**  
   Websites and products, AI integrations, and ML/analytics each receive a full product chapter: one plain-language promise, one large looping interface demonstration, three specific benefits, and a muted logo-only alternatives row. The chapters are separate rather than hidden inside a carousel. Their demonstrations are a human-reviewed responsive release, an AI workflow with attached context and a human checkpoint, and an analytics investigation that ends in one business recommendation and operating rhythm.

4. **Selected Work showcase**  
   The original three-project hero composition now lives here intact: Candella Quant as the main workspace window, The Sparkling Shoe product image crossing the lower left, and the MindLSN index card at the lower right, joined by the thin emerald thread. Each element links directly to its live product. One separate CTA leads to the detailed case-study page; full narratives stay separate.

5. **Selectable inquiry CTA**  
   “Bring us the messy version. We’ll find the first move.” The visitor chooses Website or product, AI workflow, Analytics or ML, or Not sure yet. That choice prepares a direct email with useful prompts; no polished brief or form submission is required.

6. **Footer**  
   LCG brand, consulting navigation, direct email, and “Made and maintained by LCG.” The consulting footer does not route visitors back to the personal portfolio.

Candella Quant remains identified as “In the Lab” on the dedicated Work page. It does not create a separate landing-page section.

### Work page order

1. Editorial hero: “Different businesses. One standard of care.”
2. Visual index in this order: Candella Copytrade → MindLSN → The Sparkling Shoe.
3. Each index card contains a purpose-built visual, plain-language summary, relevant evidence, an anchor to its full story below, and a separate live-site link.
4. Candella Quant remains a clearly labeled active-development/Lab item.
5. All three complete cases live on this one Work page. Each includes the need, difficult part, build, continuing ownership, responsibilities, evidence, and an evolving sticky visual.
6. Each case links directly to the live product; the page ends with the inquiry CTA.

### Why cases are separate

- The landing page stays persuasive and finite.
- Visual previews provide proof without forcing every visitor through long technical explanations.
- Recruiters and serious leads can choose the deeper read.
- Each case can grow on the dedicated Work page without making the landing-page conversion path unwieldy.

The rejected inline-case landing is no longer presented as a review option. The editorial landing and its unified Work page are the only active product surfaces.

## 7. Hero specification

The hero has three jobs in this order:

1. State the problem in plain language.
2. Show the problem and handoff through one legible product-like animation.
3. Establish proof without making visitors wait for the rest of the page.

### Current composition

- The headline and supporting copy occupy the opening viewport.
- The browser stage begins at the fold and becomes fully legible after a short scroll.
- A dark emerald, black, cream, brown, brass, and restrained purple/amber field provides color without turning the site into a neon developer template.
- The browser has a thin frame, restrained reflection, subtle perspective, and deep shadow. It is not wrapped in a laptop, monitor, or other fake device.
- The animation remains contained to one large browser so the story reads as one coherent task.
- The active beat, five progress segments, and Replay control are centered directly beneath the browser.

The hero should not revert to a grid of project-window cards, a generic options-volatility surface, a green “working” badge, or a site-wide AMCA clone. Project imagery belongs in Selected Work.

## 8. Ownership story summary

The browser story communicates:

1. A business website starts out working.
2. Its owner opens the growing set of tools required to build and operate it.
3. Notion is dragged into split screen and becomes the website to-do list.
4. Tasks accumulate and the website fails.
5. The owner opens an unbranded search tab, enters `jwlutz.com/consulting`, and chooses Start a project.
6. The tasks are resolved, the tool tabs close, and responsibility transfers without a magical logo-merging effect.
7. The original site refreshes and returns as “Your website that just works,” with only “Made by LCG” in the footer.

The animation is autonomous, timed, and replayable. It is not tied to scroll position. Full timing and behavior are specified in [`consulting-motion-storyboard.md`](consulting-motion-storyboard.md).

## 9. Conversion path

Current CTA: a `mailto:` link to `jwlutz65@gmail.com` with “Project inquiry for Lutz Consulting Group” prefilled.

The page explicitly reduces inquiry friction:

- No polished brief is required.
- A current link, screen recording, list of problems, or half-formed idea is enough.
- LCG will help determine the right first move.

Future preferred enhancement: a short four-field inquiry form for name, email, what needs to stop becoming a second job, and rough timing. Keep email as a fallback. Do not add a booking CTA until a real scheduling workflow exists.

## 10. Proof and metrics policy

Use evidence a buyer can understand immediately and that can be defended:

- Count of connected brokerages.
- Measured copy-trade routing distribution.
- Number of adaptive drills and cognitive domains.
- Complete platform migration.
- Mobile commerce scope.
- Responsibilities and ongoing ownership.

Evidence belongs with the relevant case—not in a free-floating landing-page numbers section. This keeps each claim attached to the business problem and prevents unrelated figures from reading like generic agency KPIs.

Do not lead with build time, incident response, uptime, response time, revenue, or aggregate performance claims unless they are reliably measured and materially attributable to the work.

An earlier version placed live browser measurements beneath the animated site. They were accurate but visually competed with the story, so the strip has been removed. Production performance evidence can appear elsewhere later when it is useful and properly sourced.

No testimonial section is planned yet because there are not enough strong, approved testimonials to make it credible. Real work, responsibilities, and live links should carry proof until that changes. Add testimonials only when the quotes are specific, attributable, and approved for publication.

## 11. Current case-study roster

### Candella Copytrade

- Role: flagship financial-infrastructure case.
- Public URL: `https://candella.dev/copytrade`.
- Story: a real-money system that mirrors one trader across connected brokerage accounts while making risk, latency, and execution visible.
- Evidence: 16 brokerages; 90% of measured lead-to-follower routing under half a second.
- Responsibilities: product/interface design, broker execution architecture, payments, accounts, monitoring, and analytics.

### MindLSN

- Role: adaptive product and collaborative design case.
- Public URL: `https://mindlsn.com`.
- Story: cognitive training with per-game ELO behavior, a weighted Mind Index, and integrity/anti-cheat systems.
- Evidence: 7 adaptive drills; 5 domains.
- Responsibilities: website/product design, adaptive ELO architecture, integrity systems, and collaborative product development.

### The Sparkling Shoe

- Role: commerce migration and growth case.
- Public URL: `https://thesparklingshoe.com`.
- Story: Squarespace to Shopify, product presentation, mobile commerce, analytics, and social-growth support.
- Evidence: complete platform migration and a mobile-first shopping flow.
- Risk: the live site has since changed and may no longer represent LCG’s work. Keep the case under review and replace it when a stronger commerce/service-business case is available.

### Candella Quant

- Role: “In the Lab,” not a finished case study.
- Public URL: `https://candella.dev/quant`.
- Story: a modular workspace for live charts, strategy editing, backtests, and AI-assisted research.
- It must remain labeled closed beta/active development until the product and evidence are ready.

## 12. Visual and interaction standards

- Railway is the primary reference for coherence, product-like motion, progress indicators, restrained repetition, and explaining offerings against familiar alternatives.
- AMCA contributes editorial confidence and meaningful spatial change, but the site should not become a scroll-controlled spectacle.
- Supabase contributes technical credibility and the use of authentic interfaces.
- In the Brand Lab contributes visual-first case-study pacing and a separate work page.
- The palette is “old money” rather than bright startup green: black, deep emerald, cream, leather brown, muted brass, with limited purple and amber accents.
- The page thread is a persistent progress motif inspired by Railway’s train line. It should connect the page without becoming a literal illustration or being forced into every logo.
- Do not place arrows on ordinary text links. Use direct labels and subtle underlines/state changes.
- Motion should be purposeful, play only while visible, and provide reduced-motion behavior.

Full rules and tokens are in [`consulting-design-system.md`](consulting-design-system.md).

## 13. Personal portfolio follow-on

The consulting site is the first priority because it was materially behind the personal portfolio. The personal site at `jwlutz.com` should later receive an update and facelift that shares the new level of craft without becoming an LCG clone.

Likely personal-portfolio work:

- Refresh project copy, links, and screenshots.
- Lead with real live work such as Candella Quant, The Sparkling Shoe, and MindLSN where the visuals still represent the project.
- Develop one technical hero-quality visualization from an owned project: a Micrograd backward pass, a Makemore training/generation sequence, or the dance inventory workflow from product photograph to extracted record, confidence checks, and human review.
- Keep raw notebook charts and generic dashboards out of the hero.
- Use the personal site for Jack’s identity and recruiter-facing narrative; keep the consulting site firm-first and founder-agnostic.

## 15. Explicit non-goals

- Do not clone Railway, AMCA, Supabase, or any other reference site.
- Do not turn every section into a cinematic scroll takeover.
- Do not advertise a specific “vibecode stack” as the service.
- Do not invent client revenue, uptime, incident, or performance claims.
- Do not make tool logos links to competitors.
- Do not include a founder biography on the consulting site.
- Do not add CPU/RAM scaling theater that does not explain the offer.
- Do not use generated imagery as fake case-study proof.
- Do not create an LCG browser tab inside the story; the final client site only says “Made by LCG” in its footer.
