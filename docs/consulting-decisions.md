# Consulting-site decision and idea history

This is the complete record of the redesign conversation. It preserves the user’s decisions, ideas, tensions, and abandoned directions without treating every past experiment as current.

Status labels:

- **Settled** — use this direction unless a new decision explicitly replaces it.
- **Current** — implemented/recommended, but still open to refinement.
- **Open** — a real unresolved choice.
- **Superseded** — useful history; do not restore by default.
- **Future** — relevant follow-on work outside the current consulting launch.

## 1. Project priority

**Settled:** redesign the consulting site first. The personal portfolio already had a stronger foundation and primarily needed refreshed work, copy, and a facelift; the consulting site was materially behind.

**Future:** return to `jwlutz.com` after the consulting direction is production-ready.

## 2. Audience

**Settled:** the site should work for anyone who encounters the work and thinks it is compelling, especially recruiters and potential warm-lead clients.

Known networks are dance and trading, but the business should not be positioned as limited to those industries.

Implication: the site needs both business clarity and enough technical/product depth to reward a recruiter or technical peer.

## 3. Company identity and voice

**Settled:** use **Lutz Consulting Group**.

**Settled:** use “we” and present a scalable firm.

**Settled:** the consulting page does not need to mention Jack, include a founder biography, or place a personal portrait in the conversion path.

**Current:** a compact LCG monogram exists.

**Open:** the final logo/favicon. The user was interested in generating options and seeing what sticks. The current mark is functional but should be compared against at least two alternatives in the real navigation.

## 4. Core positioning

**Settled:** keep the headline:

> Don’t let your website become your second job.

This remained preferable to broader alternatives because it makes the burden recognizable.

**Settled:** describe the practice as websites, AI integrations, and analytics for businesses and people.

**Current supporting copy:**

> We design, build, and operate the digital systems behind your business—so you can keep running the business.

**Superseded copy explorations:**

- “Don’t become your own IT department.” Useful as internal framing, less distinctive as the main headline.
- “You don’t need to use AI to feel the benefits of it.” An interesting AI-specific thought, but too narrow for the core promise.
- “Your digital systems should do their job.” More corporate, less memorable.

The animation now makes the “own IT department” idea visible without needing it as the headline.

## 5. What to sell

The original service set included web design/maintenance, operations, ML/analytics, AI tools, Squarespace conversions, product design, and adjacent paid technical work.

**Settled public hierarchy:**

1. Websites, built with judgment.
2. AI that fits the work.
3. Analytics that leads somewhere.

Rationale:

- Web design and maintenance are the easiest entry service.
- A rebuild naturally leads into recurring care.
- AI and analytics show that the relationship is broader than general web design.
- Operations and ML can live inside the three categories until there is enough proof and demand for another category.

**Settled:** lead with human judgment. Every line of code is read and understood by a human accountable for the full result.

**Settled:** AI integrations should make the business run more easily, not create another tool to supervise.

**Settled:** analytics should improve the business. LCG can monitor and act or provide a report if the client wants to act.

**Settled:** use the latest tools, but judge each choice against the client’s customers, workflow, and constraints.

## 6. Engagement and pricing posture

**Settled:** do not publish a high minimum or language that discourages a person with a small legitimate task. The user would accept a $100 task when it makes sense.

**Settled:** the public page should still lead with meaningful projects and ownership rather than advertising micro-tasks.

**Current CTA:** email `jwlutz65@gmail.com` or start a project.

**Settled:** no booking link exists and none should be implied.

**Open:** add a simple inquiry form before launch or keep email-only.

**Future:** add booking only after a real calendar workflow is in place.

## 8. Case-study architecture

The user strongly liked In the Brand Lab’s case-study page and wanted a more visual formula with less text and better evidence.

Two prototypes were built:

- Cases inline on the long landing page.
- A single landing-page gateway with a separate editorial work page.

**Settled:** use the separate page.

**Former clarification:** the landing page should not contain compact versions of the individual cases. It would contain one visual Work gateway; project names, evidence, live links, and full narratives would begin on the dedicated page.

**Superseded by the July 18 polish pass:** the detailed case studies remain on their own page, but the landing page now includes three visual, live-linked project snippets. This restores the strongest part of the original hero without duplicating case narratives. Candella Quant leads, followed by The Sparkling Shoe and MindLSN; a separate button opens the case-study page.

Rationale:

- The user preferred it.
- The main landing page stays focused.
- The detailed page can use visual-first chapters and real links.
- Case studies can expand without swallowing the conversion flow.

**Settled:** remove arrows from ordinary links. Use direct “Case study” and “Live site” labels.

**Current case formula:** need → difficult part → what we built → what continues → responsibilities → defensible evidence → live site.

## 9. Metrics discussion

Metrics considered:

- Uptime.
- Incident response.
- Build time.
- Site response time.
- Revenue or growth.
- Live browser performance.

Concerns raised:

- Uptime and incident response are often outside direct control or require a real monitoring record.
- Build time varies too much by project to mean anything to a buyer.
- Technical timings are difficult to understand on first glance.
- Revenue is attractive but cannot be claimed without verified attribution.

**Settled:** use plain-language scope and product evidence first.

**Settled:** do not invent uptime, incident, build, revenue, or performance claims.

**Superseded:** the first MacBook version used live in-browser measurements at the bottom. The numbers were truthful but became visual noise and were removed during the full-screen browser cleanup.

**Future:** add business outcomes only when a baseline, source, period, and client approval exist.

**Settled for now:** do not add a testimonial section. There are not enough strong approved testimonials, and a thin or anonymous quote row would make the site feel more generic. Revisit when specific attributable quotes exist.

## 13. Hero evolution

### Phase A — project windows and ambient surface

Ideas included:

- Three project images in the hero: Candella Quant, The Sparkling Shoe, and MindLSN.
- Options-volatility surface as a hero visual.
- Project visuals from Micrograd, Makemore, or dance-store analytics.

Problems:

- The surface did not explain the consulting promise.
- Project cards and a separate story competed for attention.
- Generic or unfinished project screens weakened the opening.

**Superseded for consulting:** project-window hero and options-volatility hero.

**Future for personal portfolio:** use real project images and consider a purpose-built Micrograd, Makemore, or inventory-workflow visual.

### Phase B — connected services and failure

The early visual story proposed:

- A business website.
- Service logos for Vercel, Namecheap, PostHog, Clerk, Stripe, AWS, Google, Sentry, GitHub, and Claude.
- Lines connecting the services to the website.
- A to-do list piling up with domain renewal, broken form, and similar tasks.
- Services breaking, connections disconnecting, and the page becoming a 404.
- A cursor searching for `jwlutz.com/consulting` and clicking Start a project.
- An LCG mark absorbing the other logos and restoring the client site.

Problems identified:

- The composition was too geometric and “vibecoded.”
- It required too many independent moving parts.
- Logos and connections became the subject instead of the website.
- Shattering and merging felt magical rather than causal.

**Superseded:** full connection-map, shattering, and logo-merging version.

### Phase C — one browser and Notion split screen

The story was simplified to one monitor:

- Each service is a browser tab with logo and job.
- Notion is dragged into split screen.
- The owner writes/receives the to-do list.
- The website fails.
- A neutral search tab finds LCG and clicks Start a project.
- Tasks resolve, tabs close, the original site refreshes, and it works again.
- The client site only says “Made by LCG” in the footer.

**Settled narrative:** this is the current story.

### Phase D — animation placement and physical form

The user asked to make it more like Railway:

- Put the animation at the top.
- Let the visitor scroll slightly to reveal the whole thing.
- Add color.
- Raise fidelity.
- Make it a 3D MacBook.

**Superseded implementation:** hero copy and a CSS MacBook shared the opening section.

**Latest decision:** remove the MacBook. The hardware reduced the useful browser area, looked less refined than the story itself, and complicated responsive scaling. The chosen composition is now a large browser stage with slight depth, no device shell, and a centered five-beat timing rail directly beneath it.

## 14. Animation behavior decisions

Early preference:

- A cinematic scroll should play once per browser session and not replay every time the visitor scrolls.

Later refinement:

- A Railway-like repeating hero animation tells the story more clearly than a scroll-controlled sequence.
- The opening-a-tab and clicking-Start-a-project beat was the strongest part.
- The larger ownership story should autoplay with progress bars.
- Each offering should have its own smaller tasteful repeating animation.

**Settled current behavior:**

- Page/browser entrance plays once per browser session.
- Browser story loops autonomously while visible.
- Manual Replay story control is available.
- The story is not scroll-scrubbed.
- Service chapters remain in a fixed sequence; each product demonstration loops independently while visible.
- Reduced-motion users receive stable states.

**Settled quality requirement:** elements enumerate individually and move coherently. Tabs/tasks must not all appear simultaneously, and state changes should not jump.

## 23. Split-view direction (July 18, 2026 — from Jack, supersedes parts of the Railway reference)

**Status: superseded by the service-system pass below.** The split-view remained a useful framing exercise, but it duplicated the website service, introduced another large simulated interface, and interrupted the chosen landing flow. Its approved copy principles remain useful; the standalone component and landing placement have been removed.

Jack's verdict on the current build: it reads as a Railway ripoff. The palette is ours; the structural metaphors (product consoles, chapter rails, simulated demo UIs) are theirs. The replacement metaphor, chosen in a working session:

> We look at this (editors, logs, dashboards) so that all you see is this (a website that just works).

**Decided:**

- A split-view "swipe" section with a draggable seam is now on the landing page (`PrototypeSplitView.svelte`, content in `consultingSite.splitView`). Left pane: our side — editor, operations log, monitoring chips. Right pane: the client's side — a clean working website. First version ships with the dev-side/website contrast only.
- Over time this metaphor should absorb or replace the Railway-style console demos (service-chapter demo panels). Do not add new simulated dev-tool UI as client-facing proof; the dev aesthetic is allowed only when explicitly framed as *our side of the glass*.
- The "a text away" line is approved in its positive form: "The next change is a text away. Not a ticket, not a quote, not a project." The rejected variant contrasted with "prompting AI all day" — do not use anti-AI-tool contrast anywhere on the site (conflicts with our stated tool posture and punches at the client).
- Invented client metrics remain banned. The client pane shows state, not numbers ("New order · confirmation sent"), until a real client permits real figures.

**Roadmap (blocked on client count):**

- A customer-stories index in the spirit of supabase.com/customers — filterable grid of client cards — where each card opens an In The Brand Lab-style visual story/testimonial page per client. Build this when there are enough clients to fill a grid honestly; do not pad it with the three existing cases alone.
- The split-view extends naturally per service when ready: AI (we see evals and logs / you see a drafted reply awaiting approval) and analytics (we see queries and notebooks / you see one sentence saying what to change next).

## 24. Landing and Work reconciliation (July 18, 2026 — current)

This pass resolves the remaining section-level architecture after the hero:

- Landing order is fixed as hero/browser story → Websites → AI integrations → Analytics/ML → Selected Work montage → selectable inquiry CTA → footer.
- The standalone split-view section is removed. Its human-review and ownership idea is now the actual Website service demonstration.
- The free-floating current-project numbers section is removed. Evidence stays attached to the case where a visitor can understand what it measures and why it matters.
- Website, AI, and Analytics each use an original, content-specific looping demonstration rather than a shared Railway-derived console pattern.
- The website demo shows human review, responsive checks, release gates, and a deliberate launch.
- The AI demo shows context attached to a real workflow, AI preparation, a human decision point, and updates flowing back into existing records.
- The analytics demo begins with a business question, shows the relevant evidence, and ends with one concrete recommendation and a choice of ongoing monitoring or a clear report.
- The landing-page Work section retains the approved original three-project composition and links to live products plus a separate Work index.
- The Work page opens with a visual index and then contains all three complete case studies on that same page. “Separate case-study page” means separate from the landing page, not one route per project.
- Candella Copytrade, MindLSN, and The Sparkling Shoe each retain a sticky evolving visual, four narrative chapters, evidence, responsibilities, and a live link within the unified Work page.
- Candella Quant remains an active-development/Lab item and is not promoted into a finished case study.
- The final landing CTA is functional: visitors choose their closest project type and receive a tailored, prefilled email. Fake inbox/progress theater has been removed.

This supersedes the prior recommendation to keep a standalone landing metrics section, a separate split-view chapter, or individual case-study routes.
